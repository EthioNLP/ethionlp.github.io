The languages of Ethiopia

Ethiopia has more than 80 languages. This catalogue has {{ cat.totals.languages }} entries, following Glottolog's wider definition: {{ living }} living or endangered languages, plus 6 extinct, 2 pidgins, 2 unattested and 2 unclassifiable varieties and 1 sign language, some of those counted in more than one of those categories. Oromo's 3 varieties are folded into it rather than listed separately. Counts elsewhere on this site are drawn from this list. How the list is built.

    Where these figures come from

    {{ cat.method }}

    Why this is more than the “80+” quoted elsewhere on the site. That figure is the familiar one, from the census and from Ethnologue, and it counts living indigenous languages. This table counts Glottolog languoids, its own unit for a language variety, which is a wider net: of the {{ cat.totals.languages }} here, six are extinct, two are recorded only as unattested, two are unclassifiable, two are pidgins and one is a sign language. Those categories overlap, so they cover {{ cat.totals.languages | minus: living }} rows rather than thirteen; setting them aside leaves {{ living }} living or endangered languages, which is what the catalogue's own status field gives. Neither number is wrong; they count different things.

    Two corrections are applied to the source data. ISO macrolanguages are folded into one row rather than listed alongside their varieties: Oromo appeared four times, once as the macrolanguage carrying the whole-language speaker figure and once for each of its three varieties. The varieties are recorded on the macrolanguage instead. And a country attribution is dropped where it is clearly wrong, which at present means one entry: Adamawa Fulfulde, a language of Cameroon, Nigeria and Chad. Cross-border languages with speakers inside Ethiopia, such as Nuer and Sudanese Arabic, are kept.

    Speaker figures are not shown at present. The available sources are not good enough to publish: an independent check of the Wikidata column found five of the fifteen largest figures wrong, one of them sourced to a blog post, and several others were worldwide counts rather than counts of speakers in Ethiopia. The alternative, the 2007 census, is citable but nearly two decades old, and Ethiopia's next census has been postponed with no date. Ethnologue has current numbers and its licence forbids republishing them. The column will be restored when a reliable source is available.

    The paper counts are indexed works matching the language name in a title-and-abstract search, intersected with the natural-language-processing concept. Short or ambiguous names (Afar, Kafa, Harari, Me'en) over-match badly, and those are marked with a ≈. Read them as upper bounds.

    Correcting an entry

    This file is generated, so corrections go upstream: fix the language in Glottolog or Wikidata and the next sync picks it up, which helps other projects using those sources, not just this page. For anything the upstream sources cannot express, or if a language is missing entirely, open an issue.
